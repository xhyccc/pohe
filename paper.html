RESEARCH AGENDA / H1—H3

From position to testable hypotheses.

Traditional software engineering was led by people; the agent harness increasingly evolves itself. Three questions follow. What is the asset when a prompt or code snapshot may be replaced within days? Must harness engineering keep the separation of concerns—and the boundary—that design patterns and POSA used to mark what rarely changes versus what may be derived, generalized, or customized? And does self-improvement with explicit variation boundaries drift less, regress less, recover faster, and attribute changes more clearly than unconstrained revision? Each question keeps its proposed answer, evidence, and falsification boundary in view.

H1
ASSET QUESTION

What is the asset when the harness keeps rewriting itself?

Question

A prompt or code snapshot may be evolved and replaced within days. What, then, is the durable asset of harness engineering?

Our answer

The durable asset is an inspectable design commitment—not code or prompts: pointcut + contract + permitted variation + test obligation + negative control.

Metrics

  • Contract retention: protected obligations that remain explicit and satisfied after rewriting.
  • Contract drift: unauthorized semantic change across successive versions.

Evidence status

Conceptual + exploratory detection/source evidence. Existing support can motivate and locate commitments; it does not establish their utility.

Open / falsification boundary

The causal utility remains open. The hypothesis would be weakened if matched mechanism-only descriptions retained contracts as well and drifted no more under the same rewriting regime.

H2
SEPARATION QUESTION

Must harness engineering keep a separation of concerns—and a boundary?

Question

Design patterns and POSA separated what rarely changes from what may be derived, generalized, or customized. Must harness engineering preserve that separation of concerns and its boundary?

Our answer

Yes—and under agent-led evolution the boundary must be mechanized through guards, gates, checkpoints, rollback, and evaluation gates; docs/comments alone are not constraints.

Metrics

  • Unauthorized-variation escape rate and violation containment.
  • Checkpoint recovery and rollback success, latency, and evaluation-gate rejection of known violations.

Evidence status

Analytical/source support only. E-family records are candidate mechanisms, not a demonstrated comparative advantage.

Open / falsification boundary

Comparative causal benefit remains open. A controlled comparison that finds no improvement over documentation/comment guidance in containment, recovery, or violation detection would challenge the claim.

H3
CONTROLLED-VARIATION QUESTION

Do explicit variation boundaries beat unconstrained self-improvement?

Question

Compared with unconstrained self-improvement, does self-improvement with explicit variation boundaries yield lower contract drift, lower regression rate, faster failure recovery, and clearer change attribution?

Our answer

We predict yes on all four outcomes—but this is a hypothesis to be tested, not an observed result.

Metrics

  • Contract drift and regression rate per modification round.
  • Time to successful recovery and completeness of change attribution.

Evidence status

Prospective / causal-open. There is no current experimental evidence for this predicted comparison.

Open / falsification boundary

The entire causal claim is open. Equivalent or worse outcomes for bounded modification would falsify the predicted benefit under the tested conditions; mixed results would narrow, not confirm, its scope.

PROTOCOL TEASER / PROSPECTIVE

Bounded versus unconstrained, round by round.

H3 compares bounded and unconstrained self-improvement in a planning cohort of 12 harnesses. The design uses matched randomization to bounded or unconstrained arms for ten revision rounds. The frozen task distribution covers feature adaptation, tool/schema change, failure recovery, and permission/acceptance change. Contract, regression, recovery, and attribution measures should be predeclared, with comparable tasks, models, and budgets.

Planning only: no causal result. No completed experiment is claimed; this teaser specifies a comparison to run, not a result to cite.
